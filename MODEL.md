# Formal model for ICRA submission

## 1. Baseline stochastic model

A fleet generates candidate supervisory situations according to a Poisson process of rate

$$
\Lambda=N\lambda.
$$

Each situation has latent label \(Z\in\{0,1\}\), with

$$
P(Z=1)=\pi,
$$

where \(Z=1\) means that timely human review is genuinely required.

Let \(R\in\{0,1\}\) denote whether a situation is referred to a human. The escalation mechanism has

$$
r=P(R=1\mid Z=1),\qquad
f=P(R=1\mid Z=0).
$$

Independent thinning gives total human-alert rate

$$
\boxed{\nu=\Lambda[\pi r+(1-\pi)f].}
$$

There are \(M\) pooled, work-conserving human operators. In the analytical baseline:

- alerts are FCFS;
- service times are iid \(S\sim\mathrm{Exp}(\mu)\);
- true and false alerts have the same service-time distribution;
- human correctness after timely review is \(h\);
- a correct intervention takes effect with probability \(a\);
- a critical alert must complete human processing within deadline \(D\).

Let \(W\) be queue waiting time and \(T=W+S\). Define

$$
Q_M(\nu,D)=P(T\le D).
$$

By PASTA, a critical alert generated by the thinned Poisson stream sees the stationary queue distribution. The end-to-end effective-human-control probability is

$$
\boxed{C=rha\,Q_M(\nu,D).}
$$

The engineering target is

$$
\boxed{C\ge C_{\min}.}
$$

## 2. Dimensionless form

Let \(m=E[S]=1/\mu\). Define offered human work

$$
A=\nu m
=\Lambda m[\pi r+(1-\pi)f]
$$

and normalized deadline

$$
d=\frac{D}{m}=\mu D.
$$

Utilization is

$$
\rho=\frac{A}{M}.
$$

For the M/M/M baseline,

$$
Q_M(\nu,D)=\widetilde Q_M(A,d),
$$

so

$$
\boxed{C=rha\,\widetilde Q_M(A,d).}
$$

The main evaluation should therefore use \(A,d,M\), with physical units used only for empirical interpretation.

## 3. Exact M/M/M completion probability

Stationarity requires

$$
A<M.
$$

The Erlang-C waiting probability is

$$
P_W=
\frac{
\frac{A^M}{M!}\frac{1}{1-A/M}
}{
\sum_{k=0}^{M-1}\frac{A^k}{k!}
+
\frac{A^M}{M!}\frac{1}{1-A/M}
}.
$$

Conditional on a positive wait,

$$
W\mid W>0\sim \mathrm{Exp}(M\mu-\nu).
$$

Let \(\delta=M\mu-\nu\). For \(\delta\ne\mu\),

$$
H(D)=P(W+S\le D\mid W>0)
=
1-\frac{\mu e^{-\delta D}-\delta e^{-\mu D}}{\mu-\delta}.
$$

For \(\delta=\mu\),

$$
H(D)=1-e^{-\mu D}(1+\mu D).
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
Q_M(\nu,D)
=
(1-P_W)(1-e^{-\mu D})
+
P_WH(D).
}
$$

For \(M=1\),

$$
\boxed{
Q_1(\nu,D)=1-e^{-(\mu-\nu)D},
\qquad \nu<\mu.
}
$$

# Core theoretical results

## Theorem 1 — Service-time feasibility ceiling

This result does **not** require an M/M/M queue.

For any nonnegative waiting time \(W\), any service-time distribution \(S\), and any scheduling architecture in which completing human review requires the service time \(S\),

$$
T=W+S\ge S.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{
C\le rha\,F_S(D),
}
$$

where \(F_S(D)=P(S\le D)\).

Consequently, if

$$
C_{\min}>rhaF_S(D),
$$

the target is impossible regardless of staffing, fleet partitioning, or queue discipline.

For exponential service,

$$
\boxed{
C\le rha(1-e^{-\mu D}).
}
$$

For positive arrival rate in the FCFS M/M/M baseline, every finite \(M\) has a strictly positive probability of queueing, so a finite staffing level exists for a positive target iff

$$
\boxed{
C_{\min}<rha(1-e^{-\mu D}).
}
$$

The boundary value can be approached as \(M\to\infty\), but is not attained at finite positive load.

## Theorem 2 — Effective-control capacity

Fix \(M,\mu,D>0\). On \(0\le\nu<M\mu\),

$$
Q_M(\nu,D)
$$

is continuous and strictly decreasing in \(\nu\), with

$$
Q_M(0,D)=1-e^{-\mu D}
$$

and

$$
\lim_{\nu\uparrow M\mu}Q_M(\nu,D)=0.
$$

Thus for any

$$
0<q^*=\frac{C_{\min}}{rha}<1-e^{-\mu D},
$$

there is a unique alert-rate capacity

$$
\boxed{\nu_M^*\in(0,M\mu)}
$$

satisfying

$$
Q_M(\nu_M^*,D)=q^*.
$$

The EHC requirement is equivalent to

$$
\boxed{\nu\le\nu_M^*.}
$$

Hence

$$
\boxed{
N_{\max}
=
\left\lfloor
\frac{\nu_M^*}
{\lambda[\pi r+(1-\pi)f]}
\right\rfloor
}
$$

and

$$
\boxed{
M_{\min}
=
\min\{M:\nu<M\mu,\ rhaQ_M(\nu,D)\ge C_{\min}\}.
}
$$

Moreover \(Q_M\) is nondecreasing in \(M\), so \(M_{\min}\) is a one-dimensional monotone search.

## Theorem 3 — Oversight paradox

Let \(s\) parameterize increasing escalation aggressiveness. Assume differentiable \(r(s),f(s)\), with \(r'(s)\ge0\) and \(f'(s)\ge0\), and remain in the stable interior.

Then

$$
\nu(s)=\Lambda[\pi r(s)+(1-\pi)f(s)]
$$

and

$$
C(s)=r(s)haQ_M(\nu(s),D).
$$

Define congestion sensitivity

$$
\kappa_M(\nu,D)
=
-\frac{\partial\log Q_M(\nu,D)}{\partial\nu}>0.
$$

Whenever \(r,Q_M>0\),

$$
\boxed{
\frac{dC}{ds}<0
\iff
\frac{r'(s)}{r(s)}
<
\kappa_M(\nu,D)\,
\Lambda[\pi r'(s)+(1-\pi)f'(s)].
}
$$

Therefore increasing sensitivity can **reduce** end-to-end human control: marginal detector gain can be dominated by marginal congestion.

For \(M=1\),

$$
C(s)=r(s)ha
\left[
1-e^{-[\mu-\nu(s)]D}
\right],
$$

and the paradox condition reduces to

$$
\boxed{
r'(s)\left(e^{[\mu-\nu(s)]D}-1\right)
<
r(s)D\nu'(s).
}
$$

Near saturation, \(\mu-\nu\downarrow0\), the congestion penalty becomes increasingly severe.

## Theorem 4 — Constant safety margin and rare-event scaling

Fix normalized deadline \(d>0\) and target

$$
q^*=\frac{C_{\min}}{rha}\in(0,1-e^{-d}).
$$

For any sequence \(A_n\to\infty\), \(M_n>A_n\), with

$$
M_n-A_n\to c\in(0,\infty),
$$

Erlang-C waiting probability tends to one and

$$
\boxed{
Q_{M_n}(A_n,d)\to H_c(d),
}
$$

where \(H_c\) is the completion probability for the sum of independent
\(\mathrm{Exp}(c)\) waiting and \(\mathrm{Exp}(1)\) service times.

The function \(H_c(d)\) is continuous and strictly increasing in \(c\), from
zero to \(1-e^{-d}\). Hence there is a unique

$$
c^*=c^*(d,q^*)
$$

such that

$$
H_{c^*}(d)=q^*.
$$

The minimum integer staffing therefore obeys

$$
\boxed{
M_{\min}(A)=\left\lceil A+c^*+o(1)\right\rceil.
}
$$

For \(d=4\) and \(q^*=0.95\),

$$
c^*\approx1.4997568.
$$

For the fleet,

$$
A_N=N\ell[\pi_Nr+(1-\pi_N)f],
\qquad
\ell=\lambda/\mu.
$$

If \(\pi_N\to0\) and \(f>0\) is fixed,

$$
\boxed{
\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{M_{\min}(N)}N=\ell f.
}
$$

If additionally \(\pi_N=O(1/N)\), the stronger result is

$$
\boxed{
M_{\min}(N)=N\ell f+O(1).
}
$$

A bounded operator pool can remain feasible only if

$$
\pi_Nr+(1-\pi_N)f_N=O(1/N);
$$

when \(\pi_N=O(1/N)\), this forces \(f_N=O(1/N)\).

## Theorem 5 — Distribution-free burst impossibility

Let service times be iid, strictly positive and nonexplosive, with renewal
function

$$
U_S(D)
=
\sum_{k\ge1}P(S_1+\cdots+S_k\le D)
<\infty.
$$

For a simultaneous batch of \(b\) exchangeable alerts arriving to \(M\)
initially idle servers, let \(K_D\) be the number completed by \(D\). Continuous
busy service dominates the actual system, so

$$
E[K_D]\le M U_S(D).
$$

For a uniformly selected alert \(J\),

$$
\boxed{
P(T_J\le D)
\le
\min\left(1,\frac{M U_S(D)}b\right).
}
$$

Now let batches of size \(b\) arrive as a Poisson process with rate
\(\beta_b=\bar\nu/b\). The mean alert rate is always \(\bar\nu\), and nominal
utilization is always

$$
\rho=\frac{\bar\nu E[S]}M.
$$

Yet the bound tends to zero as \(b\to\infty\). Therefore average utilization
alone cannot certify any positive uniform deadline-completion probability under
synchronized demand.

For exponential service,

$$
U_S(D)=\mu D,
$$

recovering the simpler \(M\mu D/b\) bound.

## Proposition — Uncertainty-aware feasibility certificate

Expose the service-rate dependence as \(Q_M(\nu,D;\mu)\). Suppose

$$
\lambda\in[\lambda^-,\lambda^+],\quad
\pi\in[\pi^-,\pi^+],\quad
r\in[r^-,r^+],\quad
f\in[f^-,f^+],
$$

$$
\mu\in[\mu^-,\mu^+],\quad
D\in[D^-,D^+],\quad
h\ge h^-,\quad
a\ge a^-,
$$

with \(r^-\ge f^+\). Monotonicity gives the conservative certificate

$$
\boxed{
C_{\rm cert}
=
h^-a^-
\min_{r\in[r^-,r^+]}
rQ_M\!\left(
N\lambda^+[\pi^+r+(1-\pi^+)f^+],
D^-;\mu^-
\right).
}
$$

If

$$
C_{\rm cert}\ge C_{\min},
$$

then every parameter vector in the uncertainty set is feasible. The only
remaining minimization is over referral sensitivity \(r\), because the
Oversight Paradox prevents treating that parameter as monotone.

## Corollary — Finite-source conservatism

If each robot's rate-\(\lambda\) candidate clock is suppressed while that robot
has an outstanding referred alert, couple the finite-source and open systems
using identical candidate clocks, marks, and service requirements.

Every finite-source arrival also appears in the open system; the open system may
contain additional jobs but never fewer. Hence for every shared alert under
FCFS,

$$
T_{\rm finite}\le T_{\rm open}
$$

sample-path-wise, and therefore

$$
\boxed{
Q_M^{\rm finite}(D)\ge Q_M^{\rm open}(D).
}
$$

The open-Poisson baseline is conservative for this common finite-source
mechanism.

# Relationship to classical fan-out

Perkins et al. use potential fan-out

$$
PFO=\frac{RST}{IT}+1.
$$

If each robot generates one request per \(RST+IT\) cycle, then

$$
\lambda_{\rm req}=\frac{1}{RST+IT},
\qquad
\mu=\frac{1}{IT}.
$$

For one operator, queue stability gives

$$
N\lambda_{\rm req}<\mu
\iff
N<\frac{RST+IT}{IT}
=
\boxed{PFO}.
$$

Thus the classical fan-out capacity boundary is recovered as a special stability case. The proposed EHC framework adds imperfect escalation, finite deadlines, pooled operators, and correlated demand. Fan-out is therefore a validation anchor, not a novelty claim.

## What the paper does not claim

The contribution is **not** the discovery of human overload, queueing, false alarms, fan-out, Signal Detection Theory, selective querying, or Meaningful Human Control.

The contribution is the end-to-end guarantee that connects those components and yields the system-level consequences above.
